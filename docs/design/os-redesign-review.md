# Design review record (temporary — resolved into os-redesign.md)

## Judges

### Judge 1 — winner: perf

I scored the three proposals against what the owner actually asked for: a strict Cloudflare-style framed structure (above all on the home page), a 'virtual me' that goes to each section or special part and explains it, a macOS menu bar (language, theme and so on) and dock on both the site and the panel, the pixel logo as the theme, Good In naming, and a result that is both awe-inspiring and fast.

**wow** is the most impressive and the most complete macOS experience:
- the logo's L-frame on every box
- a boot moment that says hello
- Go/View/Help menus
- Control Center
- a guided tour
- /studio and /code pages

But it pays heavily for that on performance:
- up to +20KB gz of new JS, with Radix Menubar on public pages
- a dither ViewTransition on every navigation
- a monospace font above the fold

It also has a real placement bug. Its fixed guide layer has no scroll listener, so the sprite will not stay perched on a section frame while the visitor scrolls. And it dilutes the frame on the home page by putting the hero and CTA in rounded OsWindow bands. The owner demanded excellent performance and awe together, so this trade fails one of the two.

**structure** has the purest framing logic and two ideas worth keeping:
- the assistant lights the traffic lights of the window it lands on
- narration rendered on the server into data-guide-say attributes

Against that:
- its last-cell stretch CSS is wrong, which will leave hairline-coloured holes in incomplete rows
- the menu bar is header-like
- the panel assistant is just a toast
- it is the least awe-inspiring of the three

**perf** comes closest to the whole brief, including both 'awe' and 'excellent performance':
- an exact Cloudflare frame (rails, dots only in the margins, ringed markers, filler cells so rows are never broken)
- title strips whose traffic lights come on as the box scrolls into focus
- a real menu bar with language, theme, spotlight and studio clock, built on native popovers, so first-load JS actually goes down overall
- a CSS-only dock with a launch bounce on both the site and the panel
- a lazily loaded vanilla engine for the assistant that stays correctly perched on section roofs and explains sections and hover spots
- pixel avatar and icons, a brand-family strip, and services tagged by brand

Its weaknesses are fixable by taking ideas from the other two:
- Replace the sr-only narration with structure's data-guide-say attributes.
- Add wow's L-frame box motif, a boot/hello moment limited to CSS transforms, the Reduce-motion switch in Control Center, the Help ▸ Tour, and the 'Me' dock tile with quiet mode in the panel.
- Consider /studio and /code routes later.
- Resolve the Persian brand form, which should be «گوداین» or Latin, not «گود این».

All three still say the pixel files are untracked. They were committed in c3390be.

### Judge 2 — winner: perf

I checked the proposals against the repo. pixel-font.ts was measured: PixelLogo is 128–157 rects and PixelMark is 30. design-tokens.test.ts reads the first :root/.dark block up to the first '}' and compares every preset's primary-foreground. header-client.tsx imports Radix DropdownMenu on the public site today. InlineScript is inert on soft navigations. The Next 16.4 view-transitions doc confirms that Suspense reveals activate a ViewTransition.

Recommendation: use 'perf' as the base and graft the best parts of the other two onto it.

**Why perf wins.** It is the most correct against the codebase constraints and the most credible on performance.
- **Public JS:** site JS goes net-negative because native popovers replace Radix DropdownMenu.
- **Assistant:** it is a vanilla engine of about 4.5KB gz, loaded after load and idle, and skipped under webdriver so shots and e2e stay deterministic.
- **Rendering:** content-visibility is made safe for the frame markers by a padded wrapper. The focused-window lights run on a named view-timeline. The dock magnification math is exact and uses only the scale and translate properties.
- **Placement:** the assistant layer is document-absolute, so the sprite really stays perched on the frame rails while scrolling.
- **Correctness details:**
  - It is the only proposal that catches the cyan, purple, blue and red --primary-foreground regression, which would otherwise fail CI.
  - It is the only one that re-applies shell attributes after a soft navigation.
  - It gives the most complete header and dock offset contract.

**Main fixes perf needs:**
- Render each pixel tone as one `<path>` (or use `<symbol>`/`<use>`). As specified, the rect budget is off by more than 3×, and that also threatens the 1,800-node DOM budget.
- Make the logo cycle run once, as the structure proposal does, for WCAG 2.2.2.
- Replace the sr-only narration with data-guide-say attributes, as the structure proposal does.

**Why wow does not win.** It delivers the most 'awe' and the strongest brand fusion: L-frame boxes and sprite, arc flights with a trail, On/Quiet/Off modes, a tour, and /studio and /code pages. But it carries real bugs:
- the fixed guide layer means the sprite drifts off its perch while scrolling;
- a page-wide ViewTransition would fire on streamed Suspense reveals;
- the dock's ::before duplicates the translucent material;
- it misses the preset foreground regression;
- it adds about 20KB gz to public JS and runs several animations that never end.

It is the riskiest to ship.

**Where structure sits.** It is the most usability-minded: the hero brand directory with a suffix that cycles once, narration in attributes, a zero-shift status order, and honest panel width math. But its grid incomplete-row trick is broken, it skips content-visibility, it animates dock width (layout every frame), and it makes a Latin-only naming call for fa without the owner.

**What to graft onto perf:**

### Judge 3 — winner: perf

I judged the three proposals on accessibility, Persian RTL and visual quality. 'perf' is the strongest and safest base.

Why perf wins:
- It is the only proposal that keeps every non-yellow preset legible. I checked globals.css: the cyan, purple, blue and red blocks inherit --primary-foreground from :root. Both other specs would ship purple buttons at 2.68:1.
- Its contrast system is correct and complete: --primary-ink, --primary-hover, #636366 muted text, and an inset #7A5A00 ring on yellow at 4.03:1.
- Its site menus use the honest disclosure semantics of the native Popover API.
- Its dock magnification scales from --S and is mirrored with --dir, so it works at any tile size and in RTL.
- Its assistant stands in the gap between sections, so it can never cover content.

'wow' has the highest awe and the best brand-motif thinking: the logo's L-frame everywhere, the Persian search normalisation, the 'pit of this' catch, the reduce-motion toggle and the tour. It also carries real accessibility and RTL defects:
- live announcements fire on every focusin;
- a fixed-layer sprite drifts off its perch while scrolling;
- the main nav is hidden behind a Radix 'Go' menu;
- the orange-red status hues collide;
- there is too much motion: infinite spinners, and a dissolve on every navigation.

'structure' has the best assistant accessibility pattern: a button sprite, a live region for explicit actions only, Esc returning focus, and pointer-only traffic lights with menu equivalents. It also has the best colour fixes (destructive #CE2C31, warning #F76B15), the most sensible Persian alias («گودین»), and a one-shot logo cycle that respects WCAG 2.2.2. Against that:
- its row-stretch CSS is broken;
- keeping Latin as the fa appName creates bidi problems in plain-text contexts;
- it misses the preset foreground;
- it has less wow.

Recommended merge: start from perf and add these.
- From structure: the assistant accessibility pattern, the destructive and warning colours, the «گودین» naming option for the owner, the once-only logo cycle (fixing perf's 2.2.2 failure), 14px Vazirmatn for UI text and 44px coarse-pointer targets.
- From wow: the logo L-frame and corner-pixel motif, the Control Center reduce-motion switch, Spotlight Persian normalisation, the opt-in tour, and a single restrained once-per-session boot moment.
- Raise mobile dock labels to at least 12px in fa.
- Replace perf's sr-only narration with paraphrases that don't refer to the visuals, or show the lines as visible captions.
